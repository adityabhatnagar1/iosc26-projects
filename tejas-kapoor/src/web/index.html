What the detector looks for

IndicatorPtsWhy it matters
Look-alike sender or link domain (paypa1-secure.com)4Attackers register near-copies of brands.
Link text shows one domain, href goes elsewhere4Classic disguise of a malicious URL.
Link to raw IP address4Legitimate services use domain names.
Risky attachment (.zip .exe .html .docm, double extensions)4 / 3Common malware delivery.
Display name claims a brand, domain does not match3Sender spoofing.
Requests passwords, card numbers, OTP, identity details3Credential harvesting. Warnings like "never share your OTP" are ignored.
Punycode domain, @ in URL3Homograph and obfuscation tricks.
Urgency wording ("within 24 hours", "final notice")2–3Pressure to skip careful thought.
Threats (suspension, legal action)2Fear as a lever.
Lure / payment scam (lottery, gift cards, wire transfer)2Money-themed fraud, including CEO fraud.
Reply-To domain differs from sender; brand mentioned from unrelated domain; URL shortener2Redirects replies or hides destinations.
Generic greeting, many subdomains, plain http, shouting/ALL-CAPS1Weak signals that add up.

Verdict: total score ≥ 5 ⇒ PHISHING.

Limitations

  • The 10-email demo is tiny, and the rules were refined after seeing it, so 10/10 is not a real accuracy estimate.
  • Fixed word lists miss novel wording and non-English emails; short, plain-text scams sit near the threshold.
  • It cannot check SPF/DKIM/DMARC, domain age, or live link reputation.
